# engine-vs-record.md - the acceptance criteria a candidate engine is judged by, and the limitations that bind the accepted one

## 0. Same populations, same code
The engine's games and the play-by-play record's games for 2019-2021 and 2023-2025 (2022 held out of everything) are counted by the same code: the possessions check, the clock check and the downs check, and one shared counting function; regular season and postseason apart, overtime apart. Every gap prints per season with its range at 2SE, taken by game (games are the unit; snaps inside a game are not independent).

## 1. What is compared, per game
Possessions; each possession's length in snaps and in game seconds; how each ends (touchdown, field goal try, punt, turnover, turnover on downs, end of half, safety) as a rate per possession. Plays = possessions x snaps per possession, both sides; game seconds per snap by clock class (RUN, STOP, PARTIAL) and time bin (normal, last 2:00 of a half, last 5:00 of the game, overtime); no-plays apart. Points by kind (offensive touchdowns, field goals, tries, return and defensive touchdowns, safeties), each as chances times conversion. Starting field position by how the possession began.

## 2. Acceptance checks on a candidate (each prints before any possessions, plays or points figure)
a. Cells. In every cell a change touches, the engine's mean game seconds a stretch sits within 2SE of the play-by-play record's mean for that cell, 2SE by game; cells drawn from stamped seasons (2023-2025) are judged against the play-by-play record's mean over those seasons, with the six-season mean beside it. A fix whose cells miss has not done what it says.
b. Class shares. In each time bin and late-half score state, the engine's stretches per snap of each class within 2SE of the play-by-play record's, no-plays excluded on both sides; stopping events (incomplete 4-4f, out of bounds inside its windows 4-3-2a, charged timeouts 4-5-1, the two-minute warning 3-41) per stretch within 2SE.
c. Drawn spreads, not means: each cell's 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles within 2SE by game.
d. Fitted parts (the race in game seconds after each act, the fourth-down choice). Inputs at the decision point only: the state after the completed play and any enforcement already resolved; the next snap may verify a rebuilt state, never supply an input. Calibration per part on each held-out season: moments grouped into ten deciles of the part's own predicted probability, predicted against observed share, 2SE by game, misses judged against chance by a game-cluster bootstrap; a part fails when its misses exceed the chance count. Injected fault: the part's probabilities raised 30% (capped at 1) must fail this check before its PASS counts. Rounds: fit to a declared cap, the round count chosen by the lowest log loss on an inner held-out season split by whole season; the chain refuses if the best round equals the cap.
e. Leak tests. (1) An input column copied from the next snap must be refused. (2) With the fitted model and the random stream frozen, every record after sampled decision points is changed while the history up to each point is held fixed; inputs and prediction at each point must be identical. (3) A held-out season's records changed must leave the fitted model byte-identical.
f. The engine-legality stage's section 4 (engine-legality.md) and its injected faults P1-P4 run on every candidate.
g. The engine run alone from its staged model files, each file's sha checked on load, must reproduce the run's games byte-identically.
h. Budget: 600 s end to end, measured and printed; a run that does not finish is INCOMPLETE, never PASS. Over budget: speed up shared work, never shrink a check.
i. Done is bounded: possessions, plays and points per game within 2SE of the play-by-play record in every season; a family whose restore moves nothing at 2SE is closed as NOT SHOWN with its ceiling. Every expectation line carries a number and is scored by the same rule as a prediction.

## 3. Standing rules
Predictions are written before the run, each with the output that would fail it; a prediction that misses is read on its parts, never reworded. Any change from these criteria' wording stops before code and comes back as a question. Shared rule semantics first: the window readers in rules.py answer equally for Python and NumPy scalars on both sides of every time-window boundary, with an injected fault that must fail. Three times kept apart: real elapsed seconds, game-clock seconds, the play clock's state; a missing end stamp leaves the time after the play unmeasured; a negative interval is a failure, never zero. Text and flags are both evidence; an unresolved disagreement is UNKNOWN_CLOCK_STATE and never enters training as RUN or STOP.

## 4. Limitations that bind the retained results
- The accepted engine plays 126.99 plays a game against the play-by-play record's 136.73; the gap stands. A later candidate (v38, 2026-10-31) reached 136.77 and was NOT ACCEPTED on section 4 (the downs check UNCHECKED instead of 0), on the fourth-and-1 go share above the accepted engine's at every zone, and on 648 of 1,676 games drawing an unsupported continuation; its run record is not kept (README, Current state).
- The fitted parts in out/engine/ were fit in look-back mode: each season's model reads the five other seasons, later ones included. That mode judges mechanics only; it is never a forecast. A forecast refits on earlier seasons and earlier weeks only.
- Future-season information in one input: the fourth-down go share for 2019's first week has no earlier season on disk, so the nearest admitted season (2020) is used and the line says so; the count of such games is printed. The input is otherwise built from games already played before each kickoff, identical when computed from the play-by-play record alone and from what the fit saw.
- Results read before the window-reader fix in rules.py (a numpy bool that never tested true) are not measurements under the corrected definitions and are not used.
- Stamped play lengths exist from 2023; the race fits on 2023-2025 only, and unstamped snaps are counted by season and time bin, never imputed.
- Postseason periods past the first overtime (16 stretches, one game) are unchecked by the clock check because no record game reached one; the reason prints each run.
- Fouls by both teams, a foul and a turnover on one play, laterals, fumbles kept by the losing side, replay reviews, injuries and runoffs are branches the engine does not draw; each is counted on the play-by-play record.
- 2022 certifies nothing: earlier iterations tested against it and their records were deleted on 2026-10-26; it stays out of every fit and screen.
