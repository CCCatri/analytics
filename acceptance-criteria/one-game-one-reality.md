# One game, one reality (the project owner's law, written 2026-11-09 before its check's first run)

Sentences served: "His team gets about S of them, because ___" (the team level) and "Of the A throws, the receivers get Y ... because ___" (the group level).

1. A simulated game is one reality. In it, each team has one starting quarterback, and everything below him in the chain (the team's attempts and carries, the play calls, how targets spread) is computed in that reality.
2. The quarterback is a causal root, measured 2026-11-09 on the play-by-play record's stats file (six seasons, 634 backup starts in 151 team-seasons, within team-season): with a backup starting, team pass attempts -6.8 a game (2SE 2.2), carries -5.7 (1.9), targets -6.4 (2.1), the top target-getter's share -2.7 points (1.8). A backup game is a different game, so its chain is computed in its own world. (The starter there is the quarterback with the most attempts, so mid-game injuries and week-18 rest games are mixed in; the sizes are refined before they feed the chain, the link stands.)
3. Two lines that cannot both happen in one game carry different world labels and are never added up as one game. At most one quarterback can throw more than half of his team's passes in a game, so two quarterback lines on one team that each claim more than half of the team's attempts are two worlds. Checks that add lines up (the team budgets) add them within a world; a player's expected line is the chance of each world times his line in it.
4. Judged by `checks/check_one_game_one_reality.py` on the card: NOT MET while any team carries two full-game quarterback lines in one world. A card with no world label is one world, so today's card is expected NOT MET wherever a backup is priced as a full game (CAR, MIA, PIT, TEN at least on 2024 week 15).
